A day here

The rhythm is the curriculum.

Children settle when they know what comes next. Every room runs the same shape of day, adjusted for how long the people in it can hold still.

6:30 am

Doors open

A quiet arrival, a hand-off at the door, and breakfast until 8:00 for anyone who wants it.

8:15 am

Morning meeting

Songs, the weather, the calendar, and what the day holds. Everyone gets their name said out loud.

9:00 am

Centers

Blocks, sensory table, dramatic play, art. Teachers pull small groups for letters, counting, and stories.

10:30 am

Outside

Two fenced yards, separated by age. We go out unless the wind chill is under 20°F or the heat index is over 90°F.

11:30 am

Lunch

Family style at low tables, served by the classroom teacher, who eats what the children eat.

12:15 pm

Rest

Cots, lights down, quiet music until 2:30. Children who wake early get books on the reading rug.

2:30 pm

Snack & choice

Then back to whatever they were building this morning — we leave the block city standing.

4:00 pm

Slow pickup

Outside again or the gross-motor room, and a note home for every family before the last child leaves.

Sibling discount

Ten percent off the older child's tuition for as long as two or more children from one family are enrolled.

Holding a spot

A $75 annual registration fee per child, and two weeks' tuition to hold a start date more than a month out, credited to your first invoice.

Safety & health

The unglamorous parts, in writing.

Most of this is required by Ohio licensing. We would rather you read it here than find out about it on a tour.

One locked door

Every family gets a keypad code. Pickup is limited to the names on your authorization list, with photo ID until the front desk knows your face.

Background checks on every adult

Ohio BCI and FBI criminal records checks for teachers, cooks, drivers, and administrators, rechecked on the state's schedule.

Trained for the bad day

Pediatric CPR, first aid, and communicable disease training current for all staff, plus the in-service hours Ohio requires every two years.

Meals that meet the standard

Breakfast, lunch, and two snacks made on site — whole grains, no sugar-sweetened drinks, water always available, family-style service.

Allergies and medication

Care plans on file and posted inside each classroom cabinet. Epinephrine and inhalers stay in the room, not in a locked office down the hall.

Drills on the calendar

Fire drills every month and tornado drills every month from April through July, as Ohio requires, plus an annual lockdown practice.

Questions

What families ask on the first call.

Do you have an opening for my child?

Sometimes. Infant spots turn over slowly and preschool moves fast. The waitlist is free and we call in order — there is no jumping it.

What happens on a snow day?

We keep our own calendar and stay open when the schools close. We close only for a county Level 3 snow emergency, announced by 5:30 am.

When do I keep my child home?

Fever over 100.4°F, vomiting or diarrhea in the last 24 hours, or anything on Ohio's communicable disease chart. We call you if it starts here.

Can I stop in during the day?

Yes, without calling first. Ohio licensing gives enrolled families access to their child's classroom any time their child is there, and we mean it.

Are you closed for holidays?

Eight days a year: New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, July 4th, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and the day after, and Christmas Day. Tuition is unchanged those weeks.

How do you handle biting?

It is normal at eighteen months and it is still hard. We shadow, we redirect, we tell both families the same day, and nobody's name gets shared.
